# How I studied for GH-900

I used Microsoft Learn to prepare for GH-900, and I passed the exam. I did not use dumps. I wanted to understand the material well enough to answer a question even when it was asked differently.

## Using AI to understand the questions

I had AI explain the questions I was studying, including why an answer was right and why the others were wrong. Then I asked it to approach the same concept in several different ways to test my understanding of what the Microsoft Learn sample exam was asking.

The exam was significantly different from the Learn questions in my experience. I attribute much of my success to using resources to go deeper into the content. AI helped make that process less dry. I was using the practice questions to find what I needed to understand, not expecting the exam to repeat them.

## Following along and checking setup

Every once in a while, I used a follow-along video. I also used AI as a setup guide because I wanted to avoid picking up bad habits. The video gave me something to follow, and AI gave me a way to ask questions about the steps.

The videos linked in this repo are resources readers can use. I have not identified a particular video as one I personally watched.

## Making Anki cards useful

I made Anki flashcards for commands, when to use them, how to use them, and the order I would use them in. My study topics also included project boards, timelines, the GitHub store or Marketplace, profile settings, two-factor authentication, and other security features.

The cards in this repo follow that practical focus. They are newly written study material, not an export of my original deck or a copy of Microsoft's assessment. They ask what a feature does and why it fits the situation.

## A way to use this repo

Read a section in Microsoft Learn. Explain it in your own words, then try a small task in a practice repository. Ask AI for a new scenario about that concept, answer before seeing the explanation, and check the explanation against the linked documentation. Make a card for the distinction you missed.

For a command, ask yourself where you run it, what it changes, what needs to happen first, and how you will know it worked. For permissions, identify who needs access and what they need to do. For Projects, explain which item or field changes and what triggers that change.

My experience is one person's preparation, not a promise of a passing score. This guide does not include actual exam questions, recalled questions, an invented exam score, or a study-time claim.
