# Choose the next correction

[Workshop menu](README.md)

## Situation

A fictional guide has three problems: a broken lab link, a vague heading, and an example that incorrectly claims a passing link check proves factual accuracy.

## Decide first

Rank the corrections and explain the effect on a reader.

<details>
<summary>Worked explanation</summary>

Correct the misleading claim because it teaches the wrong conclusion. Repair the broken link so the lab can be reached. Then clarify the heading. Another order may be reasonable if a specific reader is blocked; explain that context.

</details>

## Try it

Draft a correction for the claim: “The check verifies the local file links it examines. The explanation still needs factual review.” Then describe how you would verify a repaired link.

Finish check: Your priority reflects reader impact, and each proposed fix has a concrete verification step.

## Change the situation

If the broken link is the only route to a required lab, what changes? Its immediate impact increases; fix it promptly while tracking the content correction.

These are original practice situations, not recalled exam questions or claims about the author's experience.
